How we worked it out

6 hours back, every week.

Someone who cooks their own meals and tracks macros spends about 9 hours a week on food. Following a Coast meal plan takes about 3. Here is every number, and where it comes from.

Without the app: about 9 hours a week

Low-end figures throughout, for someone who cooks most meals and logs their food.

Where the time goesFigure usedHours a week
Cooking and cleaning up51 minutes a day, Australian men who cook (ABS) 16.0
Grocery shoppingOne trip a week, 56 minutes door to door 20.9
Deciding what to eat5 minutes a day, the most common answer 20.6
Logging food in a tracking app14.6 minutes a day, after six months of practice 31.7
Total9.2

With Coast: about 3 hours a week

We ran the app's meal planner for 12 test users over 8 weeks (84 weekly plans) and added up the hands-on minutes: the weekly cook, splitting it into tubs, and breakfasts and snacks during the week. Slow cooker and oven time doesn't count, since you're not standing there.

Where the time goesFigure usedHours a week
Hands-on cookingMedian of the 12 test users, including reheating and sides1.7
Cleaning upOne cook day of pots instead of every night (our estimate)0.5
Grocery shoppingOne trip, same as above0.9
Deciding what to eatThe plan decides0
Logging foodNothing to log. The day is already planned to your macros0
Total3.1

9.2 − 3.1 = 6.1 hours

That uses the low-end figure for every line. Using typical figures instead of the low end (59 minutes of cooking a day, two shops a week, 10 minutes deciding, 23 minutes logging), the saving is closer to 9 hours.

What this doesn't cover

  • The cooking figure comes from everyone who cooks, including people cooking for a family. Cooking for one can be quicker, which is one reason we use the low end.
  • Recipe times are the app's estimates, not stopwatch timings. A first-time cook will be slower.
  • The 12 test users are mostly people eating two tubs a day. Your week depends on how many meals you batch and which breakfasts you pick.
