The decisions

Your routeLose about 0.5% of your weight a weekLosing slowly keeps more muscle. In one trial, athletes who lost slowly even added some.
Strong

What the studies found

Elite athletes who lost about 0.7% of their weight a week gained 2.1% lean mass and lost 31% of their fat. Those losing 1.4% a week lost 21% of their fat and their lean mass did not change. Across training studies, an energy deficit held back lean-mass gains, though strength still improved.

What it means for you

0.4 kg a week is 0.5% of your 77 kg, inside the 0.5 to 1% a week these studies and contest-prep guidance support.

Sources

  • Garthe et al. 2011 Effect of two different weight-loss rates on body composition and strength and power-related performance in elite athletesInt J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab · RCT, 24 elite athletes · abstract checked
  • Murphy & Koehler 2022 Energy deficiency impairs resistance training gains in lean mass but not strength: A meta-analysis and meta-regressionScand J Med Sci Sports · meta-analysis and meta-regression of RCTs · abstract checked
  • Helms et al. 2014 Evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: nutrition and supplementationJ Int Soc Sports Nutr · narrative review · abstract checked

Your routeThe faster pace needs fat to spareFat stores can only release so much energy a day. Diet harder than that and the gap comes out of muscle.
Estimate

What the studies found

A model built from underfeeding studies puts the most the fat store can supply at about 290 kJ (69 kcal) per kg of fat per day, and says a deficit beyond that takes fat-free mass straight away. It is a model, not a trial, so we plan under it, at 48.5 kcal per kg of fat.

What it means for you

With the fat you carry, the deepest deficit we plan is about 650 kcal a day, so the fastest pace offered to you is 0.5% a week.

Sources

  • Alpert 2005 A limit on the energy transfer rate from the human fat store in hypophagiaJ Theor Biol · theoretical model from published underfeeding data · abstract checked

Your routeRecomp keeps the deficit under 500 kcalIn training studies, a deficit of about 500 kcal a day was enough to stop muscle gains.
Strong

What the studies found

A meta-regression of resistance-training trials found an energy deficit of about 500 kcal a day prevented lean-mass gains, and the authors advise against deficits larger than that when muscle is the goal. Strength gains were not affected by the deficit.

What it means for you

Your recomp runs about 450 kcal under maintenance, below that line, so building muscle while losing fat is still realistic.

  • Murphy & Koehler 2022 Energy deficiency impairs resistance training gains in lean mass but not strength: A meta-analysis and meta-regressionScand J Med Sci Sports · meta-analysis and meta-regression of RCTs · abstract checked

Your routeEase in at about 0.5% a weekIf you already train, a small deficit can trim fat while your muscle keeps growing.
Estimate

What the studies found

In a 10-week trial of 30 trained people on high protein, a moderate deficit lost 2.9 kg of fat and gained 1.0 kg of lean mass, while eating at maintenance lost 1.4 kg of fat and gained the same lean mass. The fat-loss difference between the two groups was not statistically significant.

What it means for you

You chose to ease in, so the scale moves about 0.4 kg a week. This is one small trial, so treat the pace as a best estimate.

Sources

  • Vargas-Molina et al. 2026 Comparison of two nutritional protocols in body re-composition of resistance-trained participantsEur J Appl Physiol · RCT, 30 resistance-trained participants · abstract checked

What the studies found

Across six studies, men with moderate muscularity were rated most attractive. When observers set fat and muscle on male bodies themselves, they chose levels within the healthy range. Your goal of 13% is measured against that band. These are ratings of photos by mostly Western raters, not a promise about real life.

Your routeBuild about 0.10 kg of muscle a weekMuscle grows slowly and slows each year you train, and people differ, so the time is a range.
Estimate

What the studies found

In 49 trials, resistance training alone added about 1.1 kg of lean mass over roughly 13 weeks, and extra protein added about 0.3 kg more. Men gain more muscle in absolute terms, but women gain about the same relative to the muscle they start with. How fast gains slow over the years is our assumption, not a measurement.

What it means for you

From your training history and frame, about 0.10 kg a week, give or take about a third.

Even a DXA scan, repeated on consecutive days in 21 trained athletes, varied by about 1.3 kg of fat mass, and home body-fat scales are less precise still. In one man weighed daily for 26 years, weight varied by about 0.5% from one day to the next, small enough for a trend to show real change.

In the code: src/utils/workoutGenerator/data/rir.json

Training2 to 3 min rests on the big liftsResting more than a minute keeps your reps up. Past about 90 seconds, growth evens out.
Good

What the studies found

A meta-analysis of 9 studies found a small growth benefit to resting more than 60 seconds, and no clear difference past 90. In trained men, 3-minute rests built more strength and thigh muscle than 1-minute rests. For maximum strength in trained lifters, a review favours rests over 2 minutes.

What it means for you

Your pace rests the big lifts for 2 to 3 minutes. A quicker pace trades a little growth for time.

What the studies found

Across 49 trials, protein added about 0.3 kg of lean mass to training, with no further gain above about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day (the top of its confidence range was 2.2). When lean athletes cut calories, a review of 6 studies put needs higher, at 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of lean mass.

170 g is about 2.2 g per kg of your weight. It rises as you get leaner on a cut, and never goes down.

In the code: src/utils/nutritionMacros.ts phaseCaloricTarget

MealsNever below 1,950 kcalEating too little for your training disrupts hormones, so calories never drop under a floor set by your lean mass.
Estimate

What the studies found

In 29 regularly menstruating women, hormone pulses were normal at 30 kcal per kg of lean mass a day and disrupted below it. That study was small, short and in women who did not exercise, and the IOC 2023 consensus treats low energy availability as a health risk for athletes.

What it means for you

Your floor is 1,950 kcal: 30 kcal per kg of your lean mass plus what your training burns. We apply it whatever your sex.
